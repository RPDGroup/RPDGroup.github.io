What we study

Research

AI-driven design, structure prediction, and molecular dynamics for enzymes and interacting proteins.

01 /
01 / Design & structure prediction

Designing enzymes and interacting proteins

We develop computational approaches for the design and structure prediction of biological macromolecules, with an emphasis on enzymes and protein complexes. By combining biological foundation models, generative modeling, and structure-based evaluation, we study the relationships between sequence, structure, and function and use them to guide molecular design.

02 /
02 / Molecular dynamics

Exploring conformational ensembles

Proteins function as dynamic conformational ensembles. We combine molecular dynamics simulations, trajectory analysis, and generative models to characterize structural flexibility and connect molecular motion with catalysis, binding, and biological function.

  • DynoM is a conditional diffusion model designed for multi-chain inputs.
  • DynoDB provides small-protein ensembles and workflows for simulation, trajectory analysis, validation, and annotation.
DynoM ↗DynoDB ↗DynoDB database ↗
03 /
03 / Protein–protein interactions

Understanding molecular recognition

We study how proteins recognize and bind one another, combining complex structure prediction, interface analysis, and molecular simulation. Our interests include enzyme-associated interactions and antibody–antigen recognition, with computational evaluation supporting the design and assessment of binding interfaces.

ABUS integrates geometric and physicochemical interface features in a deep learning framework for antigen–antibody interface scoring. The repository includes preprocessing, inference, and training utilities for evaluating candidate complex structures.

04 / Biological sequences

Learning codon preferences

CodonBERT uses a BERT-based architecture and cross-attention to model codon preferences. Its public implementation supports codon optimization and model training.

About

AI, structure, and dynamics for molecular design.

About our research

Ren Protein Design Group studies biological macromolecules at the intersection of artificial intelligence, structural biology, and molecular simulation. We focus on enzymes and protein–protein interactions, developing methods for molecular design, structure prediction, and conformational analysis. Our work links sequence, structure, dynamics, and function through reusable models, datasets, and scientific software.

We share code and workflows through our GitHub organization. Each project provides its own installation instructions, examples, access information, licensing, and citation guidance.
